  <section class="work-detail__section">
    <h2>Summary</h2>
    <p>Several nonrandom microbial co-occurrence patterns are frequently seen in foot infection specimens. One pattern containing many Proteobacteria species may indicate a higher risk of treatment failure. <em>Staphylococcus aureus</em> rarely co-occurs with other staphylococci.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="work-detail__section">
    <h2>Contribution and significance</h2>
    <p>This was the first analysis to ask whether the organisms found together in polymicrobial foot infections co-occur nonrandomly. Because the research question was novel, the work brought together clinical, microbiological, and biostatistical expertise. The analysis used observed-to-expected ratios, Markov and conditional random fields, and factor analysis to identify recurring patterns.</p>
    <p>Polymicrobial pattern C was associated with treatment failure, giving the finding direct clinical prognostic significance. The infrequent co-occurrence of <em>Staphylococcus aureus</em> with non-aureus staphylococcal species also raises the possibility that MRSA and MSSA coverage may not always be necessary when these non-aureus species have been identified.</p>
  </section>
